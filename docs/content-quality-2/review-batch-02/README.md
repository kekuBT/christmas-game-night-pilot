## Audience and tone: how the new mix serves Mchezo

**Batch 01 was too academic.** With the new tone tags, 14 of its 20 items were "classic" knowledge only:
- Mozart, Rousseau, Indian cinema in 1913, coastlines, dwarf planets, Sherlock Holmes and Casablanca.
- Only 2 items touched anything after 2000.

That suits a quiz night, not a Gen Z and millennial party.

**Batch 02 changes the balance** (tags overlap, so the rows don't sum to 21):

| Tone | Batch 01 | Batch 02 | Owner's target for Mixed Party Night |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic knowledge only | 14 of 20 | 7 of 21 | — |
| Any classic or reasoning content | 16 | 9 (43%) | about 30% |
| Modern entertainment, internet, music or gaming | 4 | 11 (52%) | about 30% |
| 2000s and 2010s nostalgia | 2 | 6 (29%) | about 20% |
| Funny, weird or absurd-but-true | 4 | 8 (38%) | about 20% |

The tags are not mutually exclusive: "Minecraft's Creeper was meant to be a pig" is modern **and** weird. So the percentages are a guide, not a quota, as you asked.

**What a round now feels like.** One question makes you think: Q appears in no US state name, trees are built from air. The next brings back a memory: the Wii, 151 Pokémon, the Ice Bucket Challenge summer. Another starts an argument: who really said "let them eat cake"? Another gets a laugh: a 19-second video about elephant trunks started YouTube, and Japan does KFC for Christmas.

**New items (9).** Each has a source, and the facts behind them were checked against the web on 2026-11-07. That was an AI check, not a human one.
- Gaming: `trv-cq2-21` (Creeper/pig), `cls-cq2-22` (151 Pokémon), `year-cq2-21` (Wii, 2006).
- Internet: `trv-cq2-22` (YouTube's first video), `year-cq2-22` (Ice Bucket Challenge, 2014).

**Guarding the standard on modern content:**
- No slang is forced in.
- No gossip.
- Nothing depends on a current view count or a "still the record" claim. For example, the "Old Town Road" item asks about its 2019 run and names the 2024 tie.
- Every pop-culture fact cites where it can be checked.
- No lyrics are quoted, and no quotations are invented.
- Difficulty is mixed: Spirited Away is an easy opener; the Creeper and YouTube questions are medium.

## Round-variety rule (proposal E10, not wired into selection)

The rule is `roundVarietyProblems` in `scripts/content-quality/pilot-lib.ts`, with tests in `tests/content-pilot.test.ts`. Within one quiz round:
1. No mechanic appears more than twice, and never twice in a row.
2. At most one "quote twist" question (`never-said`, `misquote` or `misattribution`).
3. At most one question whose stem hinges on "never" or "not".

The batch-01 pattern it catches: Casablanca ("never actually spoken"), Sherlock ("never says") and the US-states letter ("does not appear") all in one round. The test confirms all three problems are flagged.

Turning this into a selection rule would change which items appear together, so it waits for your approval.
